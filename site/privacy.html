Privacy Policy

Last updated 2 November 2026

Zeolite is an attendance tracker for students, published by Soumoparno Roy ("we", "us"), who decides how the information described here is used and is responsible for it. This policy describes what the app does with your information, why, who else can see it, where it is processed, how long it is kept and how to have it deleted. It applies wherever you live, including in the European Union and the United Kingdom.

Zeolite works without an account, and in that state nothing you enter leaves your device. Everything that can leave it is either a feature you turn on or something you agreed to when the app first asked, and each is described below.

When the app first opens

Before anything else, Zeolite asks whether you are 18 or older.

  • If you are 18 or older, it then asks two separate questions, with neither answer chosen for you: whether to share usage statistics, and whether to send crash reports. Until you answer yes, neither is collected; the app is set up so that nothing is sent before you choose.
  • If you are under 18, Zeolite stays on your device. See Children and teenagers.

You can change all three answers later in Settings → Privacy choices.

If you sign in

Signing in creates a Zeolite account so your attendance survives a lost phone and stays the same on a second device. It is optional, off until you choose it, and it never gates recording attendance.

Your email address
To identify your account and let you sign back in. Held in Firebase Authentication, operated by Google.
Your name, if you sign in with Google
Google tells Zeolite your email address and basic profile information, including the name on your Google account, so the account can be created. Zeolite never sees your Google password.
Your subjects, timetable, attendance marks, categories, rooms, tags, holidays, and the app settings that shape your term
To back them up and keep two devices in step. Held in Cloud Firestore, operated by Google, under a record keyed to your account's ID.

Your records are stored under your own account and the security rules permit only that account to read or write them. Google processes and stores the data as the infrastructure provider under its own terms. We can technically reach the stored records through the project's administrative console, and do so only where necessary to operate or repair the service.

Usage statistics, if you agree

If you answer yes to Share usage statistics, Zeolite uses Firebase Analytics, from Google, to count how the app is used so we can tell whether it works for people. If you answer no, or turn it off later, nothing is collected.

What it records is how the app is used, not what you put into it: when the app is opened, which of its screens you visit, that a feature was used at all — that a class was marked, that a sync ran, that a backup was saved — how long sessions last, the app version, and coarse device and country information derived from your connection. It never includes your timetable, your attendance, your subjects, your notes, or the contents of any image you read.

Firebase Analytics assigns each installation of the app a random identifier, so that repeat use from the same install can be counted rather than read as a new person each time. It is not your name, your email or your account, and it is not an advertising identifier. Zeolite sets google_analytics_adid_collection_enabled to false and strips the advertising permissions from the app, so this measurement cannot be used to target you. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data discards the identifier.

Crash reports, if you agree

If you answer yes to Send crash reports, Zeolite sends a report through Firebase Crashlytics, from Google, when something goes wrong, so we can find and fix the fault. A report holds the kind of error, where in the app's code it happened, the app version, the device model and Android version, and a random identifier for the installation so that one fault on one phone is counted once. If you answer no, reports are not sent, and any the device kept from a crash are deleted.

A report never includes your timetable, your attendance, your subjects, your notes, or anything else you entered. For a fault in the app's own code, Zeolite leaves the error's message out on purpose, because an error's text can quote what it failed on. A crash inside Android itself carries Android's description of the fault, which names the app's code, not your data.

App integrity checks

When the app talks to your account or asks for a sheet to be checked with AI, it includes a token from Firebase App Check, which uses Google Play Integrity to confirm the request comes from a genuine copy of Zeolite. Google receives a verdict about the device and the app's installation for that purpose only.

Reading a timetable from an image

If you use "Read from an image", the picture is processed on your device by Google's ML Kit text recognition, using a model bundled inside the app. By default the image is not uploaded, not kept after the read, and not shared.

There is one exception, and it never happens without you saying so. When the app cannot read the period times on a sheet with confidence, it offers to check that sheet with AI and tells you what that means before you choose. Only if you accept, a reduced-size copy of the image and the text already recognised on your device are sent to Zeolite's own server, which passes them to Cloudflare Workers AI to be read.

What happens to that copy: it is held only for as long as the read takes, it is not stored by Zeolite and not written to any database, and Cloudflare states that input to Workers AI is not used to train models and is not retained. Nothing identifying you is sent with it — no account, no email, no device identifier, and none of your attendance. The answer is used only to suggest classes the device may have missed, and the times shown to you are always the ones read from the sheet on your device.

Built with Llama.

You are asked every time. Declining leaves the read exactly as your device made it, and the rest of the import works normally.

One side effect is worth stating plainly. ML Kit ships a component that reports usage and performance metrics about the ML Kit APIs to Google, and it provides no setting to switch this off. It carries diagnostic information about the app and the device, such as the device model, Android version, the app's version and how long a read took, and a random identifier for the installation, which Google says is not meant to identify you or your device. It never includes your timetable, your attendance, or the images you read.

Connecting Notion

Notion sync is optional and off by default, and it does not require a Zeolite account. If you turn it on:

  • You authorise Zeolite from Notion's own sign-in page and choose which pages or databases it may reach. Zeolite never sees your Notion password.
  • Your attendance is written to the Notion database you picked, inside your own workspace. What happens to it there is governed by Notion's privacy policy, not this one.
  • The sign-in exchange passes through Zeolite's small server, which exists only because Notion requires a secret that cannot safely be shipped inside an app. It completes the token exchange, holds the exchange in memory for at most five minutes, and keeps no copy of your attendance.
  • The connection is stored encrypted on your device and is never uploaded. Signing out of a Zeolite account does not disconnect Notion, because the two are unrelated.
  • Disconnecting stops any further sync and leaves everything in the app intact.

Checking for updates

If you are 18 or older, the app asks Zeolite's server at start-up which is the oldest version it still supports, so it can tell you when an update is needed. The request carries nothing about you. Like any request on the internet, it reaches the server from your device's IP address; Zeolite's own logs do not record it.

Backups on your device

  • Manual export writes a backup file where you choose. What you then do with that file is your own action.
  • Automatic backup writes to a folder you pick, or to the app's own folder, on the same device.
  • Android cloud backup is switched off. Zeolite sets allowBackup="false" and excludes itself from device-to-device transfer, so the operating system does not copy your data to Google Drive or to a new phone.

Render
Hosts Zeolite's small server, which handles the Notion sign-in exchange, passes an image to Cloudflare when you accept an AI check, and answers the update check. It keeps none of it.

No one else. Your data is not sold, rented, or shared for advertising, and there is no tracking of you across other apps or websites.

Where it is processed: the records in your account are stored in Cloud Firestore in Google's Mumbai, India region (asia-south1), and Zeolite's server runs on Render in Singapore. Everything else above, including your sign-in details in Firebase Authentication, is processed on the providers' own servers, which may be in the United States and other countries outside the one you live in, where data protection law may differ from yours. Google's processing for Firebase and Google Analytics is covered by its data processing terms, which we have accepted.

How long it is kept

  • Usage statistics are kept for up to two months after you last use the app, then deleted by Google.
  • Crash reports are kept by Google for 90 days.
  • Data on your device stays until you delete it or uninstall the app.
  • Data in your account stays until you delete it or delete the account. There is no separate archive: deleting removes the records rather than hiding them.
  • The Notion sign-in exchange is held for at most five minutes, and an image sent for an AI check only for as long as the read takes.

How to delete it

  • On the device — Settings → Your data → Reset everything clears all subjects and history. Uninstalling removes the database with it.
  • Your account — Settings → Account → Delete my account removes the account and everything stored under it.
  • Without the app installed — see deleting your account.
  • Notion — disconnecting stops further sync. Records already written to your workspace are yours to delete in Notion.

Your rights

You may:

  • access the personal data held about you and get a copy of it;
  • export your records, which you can do yourself with Settings → Your data → Export backup;
  • have it corrected, which for your records you can do in the app;
  • have it deleted, as described above;
  • withdraw your consent to usage statistics or crash reports at any time, in Settings → Privacy choices;
  • complain to your local data protection authority if you believe your data has been mishandled. In India that is the Data Protection Board of India; in the European Union, the supervisory authority where you live; in the United Kingdom, the Information Commissioner's Office.

For anything you cannot do in the app, write to the address below and we will reply within 30 days. If you are in India, you may raise a grievance at the same address.

Children and teenagers

Zeolite is not meant for children under 13.

If you are 13 to 17, Zeolite works in on-device mode, and we collect no personal data from you. Signing in to an account, connecting Notion and the AI check are not available, the app does not check for updates with its server, and no usage statistics or crash reports are sent, whatever was chosen before. Everything you enter stays on your device. The one thing that still leaves it is the ML Kit diagnostic report described above, if you read a timetable from an image; it carries nothing you entered, and the identifier sent with it is not meant to identify you.

If you believe a child under 13 is using Zeolite with an account, write to the address below and the account will be deleted.

Security

Traffic between the app and the services above is encrypted in transit. Stored records are held in Google's infrastructure and are restricted by rules that permit only your own account to read or write them. No system is perfect, and this policy does not promise one.
